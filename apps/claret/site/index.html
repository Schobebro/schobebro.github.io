Claret

An offline study course for WSET® Level 2 candidates: short illustrated lessons, questions in the exam’s own format, a tasting trainer and timed mock exams, with nothing to connect to and nothing to sign up for.

The mock exams are set like the real one: 50 questions in 60 minutes, marked against the 55% pass mark.

Who it is for

It is written for WSET® Level 2 candidates who want to pass, not to browse: people who work in restaurants, bars and wine shops, and anyone booked on a course or about to book one. If you never sit the exam and simply want the subject in order, the course works just as well; leave the mock exams alone.

It is built for the time you actually have.

  • A few minutes in a queue: one review session.
  • A train journey: a lesson and its quiz.
  • An hour on a Sunday: a mock exam and its review.
  • The night before: a drill on your weakest area.

It is not preparation for a tasting exam at a higher level. That needs real wine and an examiner.

How it works

Lessons

Short cards, one idea each, with a photograph or a drawing where a picture teaches something. A quick check has to be answered before you move on.

A quiz closes each lesson, and a lesson counts as mastered only when your quiz score and your later review answers say so, not when you have scrolled past it.

Questions in the exam’s format, and mock exams

Four options and one correct answer. After you answer, the app marks your choice and the correct one with a word and a symbol as well as a colour, explains why, names the card the question came from, and puts a miss into your review queue.

A mock exam is 50 questions in 60 minutes, drawn across the exam areas in the weights the exam uses. An answer sheet shows what you have answered and flagged, and takes you to any question. You can pause; the real exam does not, so the questions stay hidden until you resume. The results show where you lost the most marks.

The tasting trainer

The tasting method, one step at a time: appearance, nose, palate and conclusions, with the Level 2 words as large chips you tap. Each case describes a wine in plain prose, and your calls are marked row by row; a call one step away earns half credit.

It teaches the method and its vocabulary. Calibrating your palate needs a glass in your hand.

Maps and labels

Outline maps of the wine regions, drawn from open map data: find a place on the outline, or name the region that is highlighted. Fictional labels, drawn for this course, teach you to read origin, style and quality terms the way the exam asks.

Review, and a plan for the days you have

A review queue brings back what you missed and what is due, spaced out so that it stays, and capped so that no day turns into a mountain. Set your exam date and the Today screen chooses the one next thing for you; close to the exam it turns to a mock a day and a drill on your weakest area.

About the course

  • A study guide, not a prediction. The app shows how ready you look, and says plainly that it is a guide and not a forecast of your result. Only a mock exam under the clock comes close.
  • Independent. Claret contains no WSET text, questions or logos, and its tasting grid is its own design.
  • Map data. The maps contain information from OpenStreetMap, which is made available under the Open Database License.

Privacy policy

Read the full privacy policy for the app and this website.

No data collected

Claret collects no data. It has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and it contains no third-party code that could do any of these.

What stays on your device

Your progress, answers, review schedule, mock results, exam date and settings are stored only on your device. They leave it only inside your device’s own backups, if you have turned those on, under Apple’s terms. Reminders are local notifications, scheduled on the device, and they stay off until you turn them on.

Purchases

Purchases are handled by Apple. We never see your name, your e-mail address or your payment details; the app only asks Apple whether the course has been bought on your Apple Account.

Crash reports

If you have agreed in your device’s settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may pass us anonymous crash reports. That is Apple’s setting, and Apple’s privacy policy describes it.

When you write to us

If you e-mail us, for example to report an error in the course, we receive what you send, including your address, and use it only to answer you and to correct the course.

This page

Changes
